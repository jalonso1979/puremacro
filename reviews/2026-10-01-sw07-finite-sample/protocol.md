# SW07 finite-sample diagnosis protocol

Selected before running the Monte Carlo experiment on 2026-10-01. This is a
development protocol, not an externally registered analysis plan. The previous
observed-data study and its unsuccessful fits remain unchanged.

## Questions

1. Do source transformations, quarterly units or the fixed shock calibration
   explain the large discrepancy in the declared Pfeifer baseline?
2. How much bias does estimating full-sample means introduce into the exact
   156-quarter, common-window covariance estimator?
3. How does the Bartlett HAC estimate compare with the exact Gaussian sampling
   covariance, and how does the original bounded fitting procedure behave on
   samples generated by its own model?

## Design

- Four scenarios: the complete declared Pfeifer and published-mode calibrations,
  followed by the same two fixed calibrations with crr/em set to their observed
  minimum-distance estimates. The latter two are explicitly plug-in diagnostics;
  they do not provide a valid composite-null or boundary bootstrap p-value.
- 399 independent replications per scenario, master seed 20261001. Independent
  SeedSequence inputs are [master seed, fixed scenario index, replication index].
  Selecting a scenario or changing execution order does not change its streams.
- Each sample has 156 quarters. Draw the initial state from the exact stationary
  Gaussian covariance, then Gaussian innovations from the model's R/Q. Include
  observation intercepts, exclude the numerical Kalman measurement ridge. No
  arbitrary burn-in or zero initialization.
- Recompute all 15 empirical moments jointly on t=4,...,155, using the full
  156-period means and a new full HAC covariance for each sample. Select the
  same nine fitted and six reserved moments after constructing that covariance.
- Repeat all four original optimization starts, bounds, 1e-9 tolerance and
  500-evaluation cap. Use the original population covariance function without
  interpolation or a different target. Profiles and historical split reports
  are not repeated per simulation; the fitting and weighting stages are.
- Preserve every draw, including failed covariance construction, optimization,
  local-rank checks and boundary fits. Numerical failures must not disappear
  from denominators. Preserve criteria, parameters, status, empirical targets
  and full HAC matrices for replay.
- Compute exact Gaussian means/covariances of the sample covariance estimator
  independently of simulations. Compare empirical dispersion and mean HAC to
  this reference. HAC bandwidths 4/8/12 are audited on the same draws; fits use
  the declared bandwidth 8 only. The known-population-mean calculation is a
  diagnostic control, not an alternative estimator silently used on real data.
- Report fitted criterion distributions, boundary and optimizer failure rates,
  parameter bias/RMSE, and the frequency of the naive chi-square rule. Distinguish
  its application to all optimized draws from the numerically regular subset.
  Numerical regularity is not proof of inferential validity.
- Observed-criterion tail fractions are conditional on each fixed simulated
  data-generating process. Report binomial Monte Carlo intervals and bounds
  treating unresolved draws both ways. They are not uncertainty intervals for
  structural parameters and do not validate the model by nonrejection.

## Evidence and interpretation

The analytic source audit is separate from the Monte Carlo experiment. Any
post-result sensitivity must be labeled and leave the four scenarios above
intact. No normal parameter intervals will be restored because a simulation
diagnostic looks favorable. State-space Gaussian assumptions, revised data,
fixed-calibration uncertainty and possible historical regime changes remain
limitations. No package release is part of this step.

## Execution interruption

A user application reset stopped the first Monte Carlo processes before they
wrote their final artifacts. Those incomplete runs are not counted as evidence.
The same four scenarios, seed and 399-replication design are restarted with
atomic checkpoints and complete per-start optimizer diagnostics. These changes
do not alter the statistical estimator or the selected simulations. Checkpoints
authenticate settings, numerical source hashes, observed data and dependency
versions, and permit continuation after future interruptions.
