    Showcase notebooks

    Choose an economic question, run one example, and then change an assumption. The catalog links to executed notebooks and their editable Python sources in both languages. The 00 notebooks describe earlier releases; they are historical tours, not the starting point for the current package.

    Choose a learning path

    The numbers below refer to the linked catalog. The order within each row moves from a focused example to a broader application.

    Your question Suggested sequence What to examine
    How do I identify a macroeconomic shock? 06 → 07 → 14 → 17 → 28 Ordering, shock units, identifying assumptions and weak instruments
    How do I evaluate a policy intervention? 10 → 15 → 24 → 29 → 60 Comparison groups, estimands, uncertainty and overlap
    How do I forecast with many indicators? 34 → 19 → 48 → 59 Held-out observations, a benchmark, release dates and revisions
    How do I build an uncertainty index? 11 → 13 → 30 → 37 Corpus provenance, normalization and validation of the measure
    Why does household heterogeneity matter? 01 → 03 → 05 → 31 → 44 Budget constraints, distributions and equilibrium feedback
    How do I solve and estimate structural models? 21 → 41 → 42 → 43 Equilibrium residuals, identification and sampling diagnostics
    Which numerical method fits my model? 22 → 51 → 52 → 53 → 56 Approximation error, grid sensitivity and convergence
    How do tariffs propagate through production? 55 → 61 → 62 → 63 → 65; then 64 Calibration provenance, accounting, welfare definition and solver limits
    How do I check a solver against a published result? 66 → 67 → 68 → 12 Closed forms, independent solution routes, tolerances and the evaluation criterion

    For a first session, choose one of 06, 10, 11 or 34. Each gives a specific question to answer before moving to a larger policy exercise. The course collections provide separate teaching sequences; their numbering differs from this showcase catalog.

    Run and change an example

    From a repository checkout, install the notebook tools and a notebook interface:

    python -m pip install -e ".[notebooks]" jupyterlab
python -m jupyterlab notebooks

    Select the kernel for that environment. Keep notebooks/_nbstyle.py beside the showcases; several examples also use bundled files from the repository. Open an .ipynb, restart its kernel, and run all cells before changing an exercise.

    The .py files in Jupytext percent format are the editable sources. Rebuild both languages after an edit:

    python tools/build_notebooks.py 34_penalized_macro_forecasting 34_penalized_macro_forecasting_es

# Execute to temporary artifacts and validate their outputs.
python tools/build_notebooks.py --check 34_penalized_macro_forecasting 34_penalized_macro_forecasting_es

# Inspect existing artifacts without executing them.
python tools/build_notebooks.py --validate-only

    --suite showcase selects the top-level showcases and notebooks/course/; --suite curso selects curso/notebooks/. Without a name or suite, the tool processes both suites. Build individual examples first: simulation, bootstrap and Bayesian examples have different computation costs. For browser execution, consult the tablet and Pyodide guide. Local LLM inference requires a desktop engine; its mock fallback demonstrates the interface only.

    Read the evidence before the conclusion

    Check the data provenance, observation units, estimand, and uncertainty measure before interpreting a chart. Country labels and realistic dates do not make simulated observations empirical evidence. Fixed seeds reproduce an experiment; they do not establish its accuracy or generality.

    • 10 — DiD: the overall ATT needs uncertainty for its own aggregation. The event-study bands are pointwise. An interval can miss the planted truth in one simulated sample.
    • 34 — forecasting: compare predictions for later, unused observations with a simple benchmark. In-sample fit and variable selection answer other questions.
    • 48 and 59 — nowcasting: 48 holds target GDP out of bridge estimation and labels its single-vintage projection contributions as diagnostics. Exact news attribution requires two information sets under a fixed model, as in 59. Its separate simulated PIT experiment does not validate the actual nowcast densities. These are synthetic examples, not official forecasts.
    • 50 — fiscal analysis: identify the shock before interpreting an impulse response as a multiplier. Debt ratios, percentage points and decimal changes must use consistent units; the debt scenarios impose their own assumptions.
    • 61 and 62 — policy simulations: the small trade preset in 61 is hand-built; 62 inspects bundled OECD data, then separates flexible building blocks from a small synthetic benchmark solve. With an active flexible setting, solve_flexible_trade_equilibrium uses the quasi-condensed solver on calibrations of up to 100 country-sector cells, or at any size with method="quasi_condensed" (dense Newton). On larger tables without an explicit method, or with another method, it raises ValueError; allow_legacy_fallback=True returns the legacy equilibrium flagged flexible_settings_applied=False. Final-demand sourcing stays fixed-coefficient unless sigma_trade is set. Compare flexible runs with the default configuration solved at replicate_matlab_precedence=False: the default call keeps the MATLAB operator-precedence convention, whose producer-price responses are five to twelve times larger on notebook 62's 2x2 economy (about seven times on the largest response). Report nominal wages, price-deflated wages, output and welfare according to their definitions.
    • 63–65 — advanced trade: 63 audits tariff-game best responses; 64 separates a scalar toy fold from synthetic CGE continuation; 65 demonstrates Hicksian EV/CV and endpoint attribution. The historical TOT/Alloc/TariffRec decomposition and theorem certification remain unavailable. See structural validation status.
    • 66 — optimal policy: the closed-form checks show that the policy solvers implement the Clarida–Galí–Gertler formulas at any admissible calibration. By default stabilization_bias compares unconditional losses. At low discount factors that criterion ranks timeless commitment below discretion, although commitment still wins when the loss is evaluated from the steady state, which loss_criterion="conditional" does.
    • 67 — Bewley classics: Aiyagari's Table I is reproduced exactly and every ordering in Aiyagari's Table II and Huggett's tables holds, but their decimals differ by more than the notebook's numerical error. At high persistence, the choice of Markov chain moves Aiyagari's rates by more than those differences.
    • 68 — third order: matching simulated paths does not show that a tensor is right; the notebook compares every tensor with closed forms and with live Dynare output. The exact pruned moments match a closed form and Dynare's means and variances. Dynare's order-3 autocorrelations do not: a closed form and Dynare's own long simulations both reject them. Skewness and kurtosis still come from simulations.

    The notebook template connects a motivating question, equations, intuition, runnable code, output interpretation, an exercise and related examples. Use its evidence checklist when adapting an example to your own data. Copy runnable code from the linked sources, where inputs, seeds and diagnostics appear together.
